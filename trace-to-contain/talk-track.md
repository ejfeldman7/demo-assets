# Talk Track — Trace to Contain

Five minutes, one operational loop. Every step lists what to click and a line to say. The same track
is rendered in-app on the Demo Walkthrough tab.

## Scenario

A filter module fails at an automotive customer and comes back as an RMA. The question that costs
the manufacturer money is not what happened to that one part. It is whether the rest of the wafer it came
from is also bad, and whether any of it already shipped. Today that answer takes days of stitching
together the fab parametric system, MES, and final-test logs by hand.

Open on the exec framing:

> "When a bad module comes back from your customer, how long does it take today to know whether the
> rest of that lot is also bad, and to stop it shipping? We take that from weeks to hours, and we
> prove we caught the population before it reached the customer."

## Agenda

1. Frame the volume on the queue.
2. Open an automotive return and trace its genealogy.
3. Move from the unit to the wafer population, and explain the driver.
4. Test a hypothesis live against the model (what-if).
5. Get the agent's recommendation and the reasoning behind it.
6. Approve under the right authority, contain, and show the audit trail.
7. Show that it does not cry wolf.
8. Step back to the whole population with Ask the Data (Genie), then reset.

## Before you start

Click Reset Demo so the queue, recommendations, approvals, and holds are back to the seeded baseline.
Set the role selector to FA / Product Engineer. Warm the what-if endpoint once (it is scale-to-zero),
so the first live prediction is snappy on stage.

## Flow

**1. Open on the queue and frame the volume.** Land on Case Queue. Point at the metric strip and the
priority-sorted table.
> "Every RMA is pushed in from the quality system as a case. Around ninety are waiting on an
> engineering decision, and the model has flagged roughly twenty thousand sibling die as at-risk
> across the open cases. The queue is ordered by customer severity and the SLA clock, so the
> automotive returns sit at the top."

**2. Open a live automotive return.** Filter to Recommendation Pending. Open a Critical / Automotive
case tagged DEP-02 excursion lineage with a red predicted margin.
> "This one came back from an automotive customer for a frequency-out-of-spec failure. Watch what the
> engineer sees the moment it opens."

**3. Trace the genealogy in one place.** Point at the module to die to wafer to lot path, each hop
labelled with trace grain and confidence.
> "The return is the doorbell, not the evidence. In one view we trace it to a die on wafer 16 of lot
> 75, off deposition tool DEP-02, at 97 percent confidence. No hunting across six systems. And the
> grain stays honest: this filter traces to the die, but a multi-die module would only trace to the
> wafer, and the app says so."

**4. Move to the wafer population.** Show the predicted-margin bar below the guardband, then the
wafer map beside it.
> "The model puts this die under the half-dB guardband. But the map is the point. The whole wafer
> shows a radial, edge-worst pattern. That is a film-thickness excursion signature, not a one-off. A
> big share of these siblings are predicted to fail the guardband, and some are already built into
> modules."

**5. Explain the driver, and kill the wrong answer.** Open the correlation card. Compare the raw bars
against the conditioned bars.
> "Insertion loss looks like the strongest predictor at minus 0.41. Condition on time and it collapses
> to minus 0.17. That was a probe-card drift artifact, not physics. Center-frequency shift holds up
> after we strip the ATE-site offset. A yield dashboard hands you the first number and lets you chase
> it for a week. This tells you which one to trust."

**6. Test a hypothesis live.** In the What-if card, drag Center frequency down and Insertion loss up.
Watch the predicted margin fall and the status flip to escape risk.
> "An engineer can ask what if the shift were worse and score it in real time against the model on
> Model Serving. Same feature definition we trained on, served live."

**7. Get the recommendation and the reasoning.** Read the agent's card and its rejected alternatives.
Click Explain this recommendation with AI.
> "The agent reads the governed genealogy, correlation, and prediction, and recommends the
> least-disruptive action that still contains the risk. Here it is a full lot hold, escalated one tier
> because the customer is automotive. It shows what it ruled out and why. The narrative is generated by
> Claude on the platform. It recommends, it does not act."

**8. Show that authority is enforced.** As FA / Product Engineer, click Approve. It is refused. Switch
the role to Quality Engineer and approve.
> "An FA engineer proposes but cannot place a hold. The app enforces segregation of duties. As a
> quality engineer the approval goes through and the case moves to MES Hold Applied. The hold is issued
> as an instruction to MES, not a status flag in a side app. That distinction is what makes a quality
> team trust it."

**9. Point at the audit trail.** Scroll to the workflow and audit timeline.
> "Every step is on the record: pushed from the quality system, the agent's recommendation, who
> approved it and under what authority, and the MES instruction that went out. That is the objective
> evidence an IATF or customer audit asks for, captured as the work happens."

**10. Show it does not cry wolf.** Back on the queue, open a No fault found case with nominal lineage.
Read the recommendation.
> "This return retested clean and its sibling population is inside the guardband, so the agent
> recommends no action. The population trace separates a real escape from a nuisance return. The system
> holds material when it should and stays quiet when it should."

**11. Step back to the whole population — Ask the Data.** Open Ask the Data via the floating button (bottom-right). Click a
starter like "How many wafers ran on DEP-02 during weeks 25 to 34?", then follow up with "how many of
those modules already shipped?"
> "Everything so far was one return. This is the step back: a governed Genie space over the same
> lakehouse tables answers the population and portfolio questions — blast radius, shipment exposure,
> which lots are worst — in plain language. It runs as me, so Unity Catalog governs what I can see, and
> it shows the SQL it wrote. Depth on the case, breadth on the population, one platform."

**12. Close on the platform, and reset.** Open the Solution Reference tab for the architecture, then
click Reset Demo.
> "Fab data becomes governed context in Unity Catalog, Lakebase makes it operational, one model turns
> it into a prediction, and the agent turns that into an action an engineer approves. Front end to
> final test to field, on one platform, with the audit trail built in. That is the seam none of the
> point tools own."

## Q&A Prep

**Is this real customer data?** No. It is synthetic data shaped to a BAW filter line, with a
deliberate deposition-tool excursion and two test-floor confounders so the analysis has something real
to find. Pointing it at real STDF and MES feeds is an ingestion exercise, not a rearchitecture.

**How is this different from our yield or test-analytics tool?** Those own wafer-sort yield and
final-test parametric analytics, and they are good at it. This does not compete there. It connects
front end to final test to field, which those tools do not span, and turns that connection into a
governed action with an audit trail.

**Does the agent decide?** No. The agent analyzes and recommends. A human with the right authority
approves, MES executes the hold, and the outcome is written back. The recommendation logic is
deterministic rules over the prediction, population, and lineage; the foundation model writes the
explanation.

**Why trust the prediction?** Do not lead with per-die accuracy. The decision is at the population
level, where the predicted margin cleanly separates excursion wafers from healthy ones and the top
at-risk wafers are all excursion wafers. The what-if panel lets an engineer probe the model directly.

**What about training-serving skew?** One feature definition is used for point-in-time training and
for the live vector served from Lakebase, so the same computation runs offline and online.

**How is Ask the Data different from the case flow?** The case flow is the guided, single-return
decision path. Ask the Data is open-ended Genie over the same governed lakehouse for the population and
portfolio questions it does not pre-answer — blast radius, shipment exposure, which lots are worst — and
it shows the SQL it wrote, so it is auditable, not a black box. It runs on-behalf-of the signed-in user,
so Unity Catalog governs what each person can see.

## Fallback Plan

- If the what-if endpoint is cold and the first prediction lags, keep talking through the correlation
  story; it warms within about fifteen seconds and the next drag is immediate.
- If a live approval has already moved cases in a prior run, click Reset Demo to return to the seeded
  baseline before starting.
- If the app is slow to load (Lakebase waking), open the Solution Reference tab and walk the
  architecture while it comes up.
- Screens to fall back to if anything is down: the PRD in `docs/prd.md` and the architecture diagram on
  the Solution Reference tab.
